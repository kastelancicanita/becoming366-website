Why Do Some People Disappear When You Start Changing?

September 17, 2026

I've noticed something strange about changing your life.

You don't only change your habits, your plans, your routines or the way you think.

Sometimes, you change your relationships without even trying to.

People you once spoke to every day slowly become people you speak to once a month. Conversations that could once last for hours suddenly feel like there is nowhere for them to go. Someone you thought would always be part of your life becomes someone you occasionally think about and wonder, when exactly did we stop being us?

There isn't always a fight.

There isn't always betrayal.

Sometimes, nobody did anything wrong.

You just stopped matching.

I used to think losing people had to mean that something had happened. That there had to be a reason, an explanation, maybe even someone to blame.

Now I think some relationships simply belong to certain versions of us.

Maybe someone matched the woman you were when you needed to complain for three hours about the same problem but weren't ready to change it.

Maybe someone understood the version of you who said yes when she wanted to say no.

Maybe your friendship was built around things you don't enjoy anymore, places you don't go anymore or conversations you no longer want to have.

And then you start changing.

You become more protective of your time. You stop explaining every decision. You start saying no without writing an essay afterwards. You become interested in different things. You want different conversations. You stop finding certain behaviour funny. Things you once tolerated begin to exhaust you.

Your energy changes.

Not in some magical way where the universe suddenly goes through your contact list and starts deleting people for you.

Your energy changes because you change what you participate in.

And that's when something interesting happens.

Some people come closer.

Some grow with you.

Some relationships become deeper because there is room for both people to evolve.

And some simply begin to disappear.

I don't think every person who leaves your life was toxic. I don't think every ending needs to become a lesson about betrayal. And I definitely don't think we need to turn everyone we've outgrown into a villain just to make ourselves feel better about moving on.

Sometimes a person was good for you.

Sometimes you were good for them.

Sometimes you genuinely loved each other.

And sometimes that still isn't enough to make two people belong in each other's lives forever.

Maybe that's one of the harder parts of becoming.

We talk so much about letting go of old habits, old beliefs and old versions of ourselves.

Nobody talks quite as much about the fact that sometimes there were people living inside those old versions too.

And when you leave that version of yourself behind, not everyone comes with you.

I'm learning not to chase every relationship that becomes distant.

Not because I don't care.

But because I no longer believe that every connection has to be preserved simply because it once mattered.

Some people are part of your whole story.

Some are part of a chapter.

And a chapter doesn't become meaningless just because it ended.

Maybe growing isn't always about finding new people who match your energy.

Sometimes it's simply about noticing when you're forcing yourself to match an energy that no longer feels like yours.
