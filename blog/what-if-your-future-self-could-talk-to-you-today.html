Becoming Her

What If Your Future Self Could Talk to You Today?

September 12, 2026

You don't need to know exactly how your life will change. Sometimes, you just need to meet the woman who kept going.

There is a version of you three years from now who remembers this exact season of your life.

She remembers the mornings when you woke up worried about money. She remembers checking your phone, hoping for a sale, an email, a sign that something was finally working. She remembers working much harder than the results seemed to justify. She remembers trying to fix her habits, her body, her finances, her work, her thoughts and her future all at the same time, and wondering whether she was wasting her time.

And she remembers getting up the next morning anyway.

We spend so much time asking ourselves what we should do next. There is another question worth asking: who am I becoming if I keep going?

I met my future self this morning

I tried a simple exercise. Instead of writing another list of goals, I imagined myself three years from now. Not a vague, perfect version of me. I wanted details. Where does she wake up? What does she do first? What does her home feel like? How does she work? How does she feel about money? Who sits around her table? What does she see when she looks in the mirror? What happened to all the things she worries about today?

And eventually, the most important question:

Is she happy?

At first, my answers were exactly what you would expect.

I saw the house.

A home surrounded by greenery. A pool. A garden. Space for my children to play. A bedroom that feels like my own little sanctuary. I saw financial freedom. A business that grew far beyond what I can see today. Digital products selling while I sleep. A brand people recognize. Enough money to save, invest, help my children, help my parents and travel without calculating whether I can afford to enjoy my life.

I saw my body healthy and strong. I saw beautiful teeth, clothes I love, hair I finally know what to do with, long walks, exercise that no longer feels like punishment.

I saw my children growing up happy. I saw friendships that survived the years. I saw repaired relationships. I saw travel.

Japan. New York. Disneyland. Islands. Trips with my children, my partner, my sister, my friends — and sometimes completely alone.

I saw love too.

But interestingly, love didn't arrive to rescue me. In the life I imagined, I met him when I was already okay. When I had built a life I loved. He didn't become my life. He joined it. And somewhere during this exercise, something changed.

The house stopped being the point.

The money stopped being the point.

Even the successful business stopped being the point.

Because when I imagined waking up as that woman, there was one feeling underneath everything else.

Peace.

Maybe the life you want is really about how you want to feel

I thought I wanted success. And I do. I want the house. I want the freedom. I want to build the brand. I want the money. I'm not going to pretend those things suddenly don't matter. They do.

But I realized why I want them. I want to wake up without fear. I want money to become a tool instead of something that determines whether I feel safe. I want to work because I have ideas, not because I'm terrified of what happens if I stop. I want to be completely present when my children need me. I want to help my parents without checking my bank account first. I want to book the trip. Buy the ticket. Take my sister somewhere we've always talked about going. I want to sit outside my own home one evening and realize there is nowhere else I need to be.

And perhaps most importantly:

I want to be at peace with myself.

Then I asked her what she thought of me.

This was the part I wasn't expecting. I imagined that woman finding something I had written today. She knows how the story continues. I don't. She knows which ideas worked. Which ones failed. Which fears never happened. Which problems eventually disappeared. She knows exactly how much uncertainty I was carrying while trying to build the life she now gets to live. So I asked her:

What would you tell me if you could sit beside me for five minutes?

And this is what came:

Hey, you. I know you wake up with doubts. I know you're trying to push away the thoughts telling you this won't work. I know money scares you right now. I know you're working incredibly hard without having enough evidence yet that any of it will pay off. But I need you to keep going. The things you're creating will find the people who need them. You will help women who feel stuck the way you once felt stuck. You don't need to know exactly how everything is going to happen. You're braver than you think.

I love you.

And thank you for the life I have today.

That last sentence stayed with me. I spend so much of my time looking at my present self from today's perspective — what isn't finished yet, what needs fixing, why I'm not further ahead, why this isn't working faster. My future self wouldn't judge me that way. She would know what today's work becomes. She wouldn't be disappointed in me. She'd be grateful to me.

And then I realized something else

I don't think I have to wait three years to decide whether I've succeeded. I've done more than I give myself credit for: gotten out of bed when every thought told me to stay there, kept promises to myself when motivation was nowhere to be found, become a mother, stood behind my decisions, cried what needed to be cried, got back up when life knocked me down. One of the bigger successes might still be learning to forgive myself — for the mistakes, the wrong decisions, the things I wish I'd handled differently, and for the versions of me who simply didn't know what I know now. I'm not sure the path forward requires rejecting who I am until I finally turn into someone better. It might require accepting who I am now enough to grow into who I want to be.

Try this tonight

Don't make another vision board yet. Don't start with numbers or ask what car you drive or how much money is in your bank account. Meet her first. Picture where she wakes up and what that morning feels like, how she feels about money, what work she does, who is still at her table, how she treats her body, what she has forgiven, what she is proud of, what freedom looks like to her — and then ask what she would say to you if she could sit beside you for five minutes. Write the answer without trying to make it beautiful. Let her speak.

You may discover, like I did, that she isn't standing three years ahead of you with a measuring tape. She's looking back. She remembers the uncertainty, the work, the mistakes, the days when nothing seemed to move, and she's rooting for you — maybe even saying, please don't stop now.

One day you might sit inside a life that once existed only in your imagination and understand that it didn't happen in one leap. It happened in all the small, unglamorous days when you kept going anyway. I don't know if my future self will have every detail I pictured this morning. I know what I heard when I asked her to speak. For tonight, that feels like enough. ❤️
