Why Do I Feel Like I'm Wasting Time When I Rest?

September 17, 2026

I read The 5 AM Club by Robin Sharma a while ago. I wish I could tell you I absorbed every lesson, built the perfect morning routine, mastered the 20/20/20 formula and transformed my life before sunrise.

I didn't.

Apparently, what my brain decided to keep from an entire book was one very simple instruction: get up at 5 AM.

So I do.

And somewhere along the way, getting up early stopped feeling like something I do and started feeling like evidence that I'm doing life properly. If I'm awake at five, reading, writing, working, planning or building something, I feel good. Productive. Disciplined. Like I'm moving forward.

But give me an afternoon with nothing urgent to do and suddenly I have a problem.

Because I don't really know how to do nothing without feeling like I should be doing something.

And that's strange, because I'm perfectly capable of telling someone else that she needs rest. I'd probably tell her that she can't constantly work, think, plan, solve, create and take care of everyone without eventually needing to stop. I'd tell her that resting isn't laziness. And I would mean it.

But when it's me? Different rules.

There is always something I could be doing. One more page. One more idea. One more task I could finish today instead of tomorrow. One tiny thing that will only take ten minutes — which somehow turns into another hour.

And even when I finally sit down, part of my brain remains standing.

I think I've realised that maybe I'm not actually bad at resting. Maybe I'm bad at feeling unproductive.

Because physically resting isn't difficult. I can lie on the couch. I can watch something. I can drink coffee slowly. I can spend an afternoon doing absolutely nothing productive.

The difficult part is allowing myself to enjoy it without mentally calculating everything I could have accomplished during those same hours.

That's not really rest.

That's productivity guilt with your feet up.

And the more ambitious you become, the easier it is to convince yourself that every unused hour is an opportunity you wasted. There is always another version of you waiting somewhere ahead. More successful. More disciplined. More organised. Further along.

So you keep moving toward her.

And somewhere in all that becoming, you forget that she is supposed to have a life too.

That thought bothers me a little, because I don't want to become a woman who can only feel proud of herself when she's exhausted.

I love ambition. I love having plans. I love building things. I actually like my ridiculous 5 AM mornings. I'm not trying to become less driven.

I just don't want productivity to become the price of my own approval.

I don't want a good day to automatically mean a day in which I accomplished a lot. Some days should be allowed to be good simply because I enjoyed being alive in them.

Maybe that's something else I should have taken from all those personal-development books.

Or maybe Robin Sharma wrote it and I was too busy remembering the 5 AM part.

Either way, I'm learning.

Rest isn't the time between the important parts of my life.

It is part of my life.
