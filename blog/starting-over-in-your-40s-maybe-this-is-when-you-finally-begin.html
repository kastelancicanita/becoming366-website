Becoming Her

Starting Over in Your 40s: Maybe This Is When You Finally Begin

September 4, 2026

They used to say that 30 is the new 40.

Now we joke that 40 is the new 20.

I'm not sure either of those things is true.

But I do think something happens when you reach your forties.

Maybe you simply collect enough years to finally notice what isn't working anymore.

And starting over in your 40s doesn't always mean blowing up your entire life.

You don't necessarily need to change continents, leave your husband, quit your job, sell everything you own and disappear to Bali to "find yourself."

Sometimes the shift is much quieter than that.

You start taking better care of yourself.

You care more about how you feel in your body.

You start reading again. Learning again. Becoming curious about things you ignored for years.

You invest in your appearance, not necessarily because you suddenly care what everyone thinks, but because you've started caring how you feel when you look in the mirror.

You begin questioning work that you once simply accepted.

You learn new skills.

You start wondering what else you could do with your life.

And sometimes, somewhere in the middle of all of that, you finally find the thing you wish you had found twenty years earlier.

I didn't find "my thing" at 20

I certainly didn't have some beautiful career plan when I was younger.

I worked different jobs throughout my adult life. Some I liked more, some less. For the most part, I was reasonably satisfied.

But none of them ever really felt like it.

Looking back, perhaps life was trying to tell me that through the constant changes.

I just didn't know how to listen yet.

Then, in my forties, I got divorced.

And in my case, starting over stopped being a nice metaphor.

I left the comfort of the life I had known, moved into a rented apartment and began building a different life from the ground up.

There was no twenty-year business plan waiting in a drawer.

I started learning.

On my own.

I learned Canva. Digital marketing. Design. Flyers and ads and formats and approximately seven thousand tiny things I didn't even know existed until I needed to know them.

Which is particularly funny because I had technically had Instagram for years.

My private account had about four posts.

Four. 😂

I was always much happier writing a Facebook status than posting Reels and Stories. I never had much desire to show the visual details of my life to strangers. I preferred sharing what was happening inside me.

And somehow, years later, digital marketing became part of my work.

Life has a sense of humor.

Starting a business in my 40s

After my divorce, I decided to open my own business.

And when I say I started from scratch, I mean it.

I taught myself the tools.

I learned digital marketing.

I learned Canva.

I learned how to create digital products.

I learned by making things, getting things wrong, changing direction and trying again.

At first, I spread myself far too wide.

I wanted to learn everything.

Do everything.

Try everything.

And now I can feel that changing too.

I'm narrowing the circle.

I'm beginning to understand what I actually want to build, what belongs in my work and what doesn't.

I'm finding stability inside something I created myself.

Would it have been easier if I had figured all of this out at 25?

Probably.

But I also don't think the 25-year-old version of me could have built what I'm building now.

She didn't know enough about herself yet.

Maybe your 40s are an awakening

There's another thing nobody talks about enough.

If you're lucky, by the time you reach your forties, your children may finally be old enough for you to sleep eight uninterrupted hours.

Luxury. 😂

You don't have to watch another human being every second of every day.

The friendships that survived have often become deeper.

You've probably lost some people along the way too — and by now, you're less interested in replacing every person who leaves.

You've eliminated relationships that existed mostly because you hadn't yet learned how to say no.

You know yourself better.

You know what exhausts you.

You know who exhausts you.

And, perhaps for the first time in a very long time, there is a little space for you.

That space can feel strange at first.

Because many women spend their twenties and thirties building everything around them.

Relationships.

Families.

Careers.

Homes.

Responsibilities.

We become very good at making sure everything functions.

And then somewhere in our forties, we begin asking a different question:

But do I function?

Because eventually you realise that if you are falling apart, there is a very good chance that everything you've been holding together will start falling apart too.

Taking care of yourself stops feeling selfish.

It starts feeling necessary.

My life changed in ways I never expected

Starting over for me wasn't only about work.

I turned toward things I hadn't explored in the same way before.

Meditation.

Manifestation.

Prayer.

I started reading the Bible intensely.

I read books that challenged the way I thought about myself, my habits, my choices and what I believed was possible for my life.

Some of those things have brought beautiful results into my life.

Not because I sat on a sofa, manifested very hard and waited for the universe to deliver a new life to my door.

I changed too.

I learned.

I worked.

I questioned myself.

I started creating.

I built a business.

I began making digital products.

I started paying attention to the person I was becoming instead of only the circumstances I wanted to change.

And I'm still doing it.

This isn't the story of someone who started over and now has everything perfectly figured out.

I don't.

I'm still in it.

You don't need to destroy your life to start again

That might be the biggest thing I've learned about starting over after 40.

Your new beginning might be enormous.

A divorce.

A move.

A career change.

Starting a business.

Or it might be almost invisible to everyone except you.

Reading instead of scrolling.

Going back to school.

Taking care of your body.

Learning something completely new.

Changing the way you spend money.

Finally setting a boundary.

Going to therapy.

Praying again.

Walking away from a friendship you outgrew.

Applying for something you would have talked yourself out of ten years ago.

Starting the thing you keep telling yourself you're too old to start.

You don't have to become a completely different woman.

Maybe you simply start becoming more deliberate about the woman you already are.

Maybe 40 isn't the new 20

And honestly?

I don't want my twenties back.

There were beautiful parts of them, of course.

But I had far less knowledge, far less perspective and far less understanding of myself.

At this age, I know more about what I want.

More importantly, I know much more about what I don't want.

I have relationships I chose carefully.

I have children who are growing into their own people.

I have work that I'm building rather than simply falling into.

I'm learning things because I want to learn them.

I'm exploring spirituality because I want to understand it.

I'm building a life with much more intention than I ever did when I was supposedly at the age when life was meant to "begin."

So maybe 40 isn't the new 20.

Maybe 40 is just 40.

And maybe that's actually better.

Because starting over in your 40s doesn't mean you failed to build the right life the first time.

It means you've finally lived long enough to know what you want to do differently.

And if you can feel that little shift happening inside you — the feeling that something needs to change, even if you can't quite name it yet — you don't need to have the entire next chapter figured out.

Start learning.

Start reading.

Start taking care of yourself.

Start paying attention to what keeps pulling at you.

Start with one thing.

Because perhaps this isn't the age when you're supposed to have everything figured out.

Perhaps this is the age when you finally know yourself well enough to begin.
