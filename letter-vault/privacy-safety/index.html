Privacy & Data

The Letter Vault is operated by A DIGITAL, obrt za usluge i izdavaštvo, vl. Anita Kaštelančić under the Becoming 366 brand.

For the purposes of applicable data protection law, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), A DIGITAL, obrt za usluge i izdavaštvo, vl. Anita Kaštelančić is the data controller responsible for the processing described in this Privacy & Safety information for The Letter Vault.

The Letter Vault is designed for private, personal future correspondence. We process personal data only as necessary to provide, protect, manage and deliver the service, comply with legal obligations, prevent misuse and respond to privacy or security requests.

What information we may process

Depending on how The Letter Vault is used, we may process:

  • purchaser email address and purchase or entitlement reference
  • recipient email address
  • recipient name, where provided
  • a sender-selected display name for non-Myself letters, used to identify the source of recipient information at delivery (this is not necessarily the purchaser’s legal name)
  • limited recipient relationship or event information where required for a Letter Vault feature
  • chosen delivery date and time zone
  • encrypted letter content
  • delivery, cancellation, bounce and failure status
  • limited security, audit and anti-abuse information
  • required declarations and their acceptance date and version
  • support and privacy-request correspondence

We do not use recipient information or private letter content for advertising, behavioural profiling, resale or recipient marketing.

How letter content is handled

Before a letter is sealed, an unfinished draft may be stored locally in the purchaser’s browser on that device.

When a letter is sealed, the content is transmitted securely to The Letter Vault and encrypted for storage.

The Letter Vault is not a zero-knowledge or end-to-end encrypted service. Our systems must be technically capable of processing the letter when it is sealed and again when it is prepared for delivery. The email delivery provider receives the content necessary to deliver the letter.

Access to production systems is restricted. Private letter content is not used for advertising, profiling, AI training or unrelated commercial purposes.

Why we process purchaser data

When you purchase or directly use The Letter Vault, we process information as necessary to provide and manage the service you requested, protect the service and comply with applicable legal obligations.

Why we may process recipient data

When a purchaser provides information about another person, that information has not been collected directly from the recipient.

For ordinary personal recipient information, A DIGITAL relies on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.

Those interests are:

  • the purchaser’s interest in arranging a private future communication with a person they personally know; and
  • A DIGITAL’s interest in securely providing that requested private delivery service.

We limit Surprise delivery to private, one-to-one communication and apply safeguards including purpose limitation, encryption, access controls, cancellation, recipient objection, suppression, defined deletion periods and restrictions against marketing, bulk communication and abuse.

Keep It a Surprise

Some purchasers choose to keep the existence of a future letter secret until its selected delivery date.

Contacting the recipient in advance would itself reveal that a specific private future communication exists.

For this limited purpose, A DIGITAL relies on the serious-impairment provision in Article 14(5)(b) GDPR where and for as long as providing individual advance information would seriously impair the specific purpose of preserving the existence of that private future communication until the sender-selected date.

Public privacy information for recipients remains available during that period.

When the reason for delayed individual notice ends, including when the letter is delivered, privacy information is provided to the recipient.

This approach is applied narrowly and is kept under review.

Recipient Privacy

Information for people who receive — or believe they may be the intended recipient of — a Letter Vault letter.

Why might The Letter Vault have information about me?

Someone who personally knows you may have used The Letter Vault to arrange a private letter for future delivery.

That person may have provided limited information needed to store and deliver the letter, such as your email address, optional name, a delivery date and limited information required by the selected Letter Vault product.

A private encrypted letter may also relate to you.

Where did my information come from?

The information was supplied by someone who used The Letter Vault and stated that they personally know the intended recipient.

At delivery, we identify the source using the sender-selected display name they provided when creating the letter. That name is chosen by the sender for delivery disclosure and is not necessarily their legal name.

The purchaser’s email address is not disclosed to recipients for source identification.

Why wasn’t I contacted earlier?

If the sender selected Keep It a Surprise, contacting you earlier would have revealed that a specific private future communication existed.

Preserving the existence of that communication until the sender-selected date is part of the purpose of Surprise delivery.

During that period, this Recipient Privacy information is publicly available.

When the letter is delivered, you receive privacy information directly.

What is the legal basis?

For ordinary recipient information that was not obtained directly from you, A DIGITAL relies on legitimate interests under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR.

The interests are the sender’s interest in arranging a private timed communication with someone they personally know and A DIGITAL’s interest in securely providing that service.

For Surprise delivery, A DIGITAL relies on Article 14(5)(b) GDPR only where and for as long as individual advance notice would seriously impair the specific purpose of preserving the existence of that private future communication.

Depending on the product, this may include:

  • your name, where provided

  • limited relationship or event information required by the product

  • minimal security and audit records

We do not use recipient information or letter content to:

Surprise Delivery Rules

The Letter Vault’s Keep It a Surprise option exists for genuine private correspondence between people who personally know each other.

It is not a tool for anonymous outreach or unsolicited campaigns.

A Surprise letter must be:

  • a private personal communication
  • intended for one natural person
  • sent by someone who personally knows that person
  • scheduled for a genuine future delivery
  • unrelated to marketing, advertising or commercial prospecting

The Letter Vault must not be used for:

  • marketing or sales outreach
  • bulk messaging
  • lead generation
  • political campaigning or political outreach
  • recruitment outreach
  • debt collection
  • scraped or purchased contact lists
  • unsolicited commercial communication
  • repeated unwanted targeting
  • threats, harassment, stalking or intimidation
  • impersonation, fraud or unlawful activity
  • deliberately harmful or rights-infringing communication

Before using Surprise delivery, the purchaser must confirm:

“I am sending this private letter to one person I personally know. This is a personal message, not marketing, advertising, bulk email, or unsolicited commercial outreach.”

A recipient may exercise their privacy rights regardless of this declaration.

If a verified recipient objects to a pending Surprise delivery, The Letter Vault may cancel the delivery and erase the relevant data.

Children & Minor Recipients

The Letter Vault can have a deeply personal use for parents and guardians who want to leave words for a child to receive in the future.

Because children’s personal information deserves additional protection, special rules apply.

For the current Letter Vault service, a Surprise letter addressed to a minor may only be created by that child’s parent or legal guardian.

The purchaser must be 18 or older.

When a recipient is identified as a minor, the purchaser must confirm:

“I confirm that I am this child’s parent or legal guardian and that the information I provide is accurate.”

This declaration does not remove the child’s own privacy rights and is not treated as consent given by the child.

What information should be provided?

Only information reasonably necessary to provide the Letter Vault service should be entered.

Do not provide identification documents, birth certificates or other official records unless The Letter Vault specifically and lawfully requests them. The current Letter Vault service does not require these documents.

Sensitive information about a child

Private letters can naturally be emotional and personal.

The Letter Vault must not be used to create a hidden record about a child for unrelated purposes, monitoring, profiling, assessment or future commercial use.

Children’s rights

Storage & Deletion

Before you seal

An unfinished draft may be automatically saved in your browser on the same device.

That draft is not a server-side Letter Vault backup.

Changing device or browser, clearing browser data or removing local storage may cause an unfinished draft to be lost.

For especially long or important letters, you may wish to keep your own draft in Notes, Word or another private document until you are ready to seal it.

After you seal

Once sealed, your letter is transmitted to The Letter Vault and encrypted for storage.

The words inside a sealed letter cannot be edited.

Permitted delivery details may still be managed before delivery where the product supports them.

A pending letter may also be cancelled before delivery.

Pending letters

Pending letters are stored for as long as necessary to provide the future-delivery service selected by the purchaser, subject to applicable law, recipient rights and service policies.

After delivery

After successful delivery, encrypted letter content and unnecessary recipient personal information are normally retained for no longer than 30 days to allow limited operational handling and then deleted from the active Letter Vault system.

Cancelled letters

When a pending letter is cancelled, its content and unnecessary recipient information are normally deleted within 30 days.

Terminal delivery failure

Where delivery fails but recovery remains possible, the letter may be retained while the delivery issue can reasonably be resolved.

When the failure becomes terminal, the applicable deletion period begins and the content is normally deleted within 30 days.

Privacy erasure

A verified privacy request may require relevant information to be deleted sooner.

Where erasure of a pending letter is required, that deletion may make future delivery permanently impossible.

Minimal records

Deletion of letter content does not necessarily require deletion of every business record.

Minimal information may be retained where genuinely necessary for:

  • accounting or tax obligations
  • security
  • fraud or abuse prevention
  • honouring a recipient suppression request
  • establishing, exercising or defending legal claims
  • demonstrating that a privacy or deletion request was completed

Backups

Operational backups are protected and managed separately from active Letter Vault data. Deleted information may remain temporarily in protected backup media until the applicable backup lifecycle removes or overwrites it, unless applicable law requires a different treatment.
